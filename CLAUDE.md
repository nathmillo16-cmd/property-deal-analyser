CLAUDE.md — Deals N Yields

Standing brief for Claude Code. Read this at the start of every session before doing anything.

What this project is

Deals N Yields is a UK property deal-analysis and deal-tracking web app for property sourcers and investors. A user signs up, enters headline figures from a listing across four strategies (BTL, HMO, Serviced Accommodation, or Flip), gets a full breakdown, a maximum bid to hit their target, and an optional AI verdict. Deals can be saved, promoted into a kanban pipeline to track through a sales process, and compared side by side. The app is a walled garden — signup/login is required before using anything — with a free tier and a paid tier (£29/month via Stripe) that unlocks the AI verdict, unlimited saved deals, and the pipeline.

Stack (as it actually is)

- Backend: Node.js / Express — server.js. Serves static HTML files, proxies AI requests to the Anthropic API (key stays server-side), and exposes all data endpoints (deals, pipeline, profile/defaults, Stripe checkout, Stripe webhook). Every data endpoint requires a valid Supabase bearer token; paid-only endpoints additionally check the caller's plan server-side via a shared fail-closed helper.
- Frontend: plain HTML/CSS/JS, no framework, no build step. Each page is a self-contained file (index.html, pipeline.html, saved-deals.html, home.html, portfolio.html, refurb.html, letters.html, login.html, landing.html, pricing.html) plus one small shared file, auth-guard.js, used by the logged-in pages to redirect to the landing page if there's no session.
- Auth/database: Supabase (Postgres + Auth), Row Level Security on every table, keyed to auth.uid().
- Payments: Stripe, test mode currently, live checkout + webhook flow both built and verified.
- Deployed on: Render, auto-deploying from the GitHub main branch (pushing to main ships to production — no separate deploy step).

THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE — calculation logic

The calculation logic for BTL, HMO, SA, and Flip has been verified line-by-line against the owner's own sourcing spreadsheets and worked examples. Do NOT change, "improve", refactor, or re-derive any calculation on your own initiative, and do NOT invent or guess a formula for a new strategy — the user supplies the exact spec. If you think a calculation is wrong, STOP and flag it to the user in plain English — do not silently alter it. Only change calculation logic when the user explicitly asks you to, and when you do, tell them exactly what you changed. When building a new calculation feature, verify it with a fully worked example (ideally by executing the real shipped code against known inputs, not just describing the formula) before telling the user it's done.

Verified calculation rules (do not alter without explicit instruction)

Shared/reused across strategies:
- Mortgage is interest-only. Monthly = (loan × rate/100) / 12.
- Deposit is calculated against END MARKET VALUE (EMV), not purchase price.
- Stamp duty (BTL surcharge bands): 3% up to £250k, 8% £250k–£925k, 13% £925k–£1.5m, 15% above £1.5m. Reused as-is by every strategy that references stamp duty; in BTL/HMO/SA it is informational/display-only and is not itself deducted anywhere in the yield/ROI/cashflow maths (this looks unusual but is the verified existing behavior — do not "fix" it).

BTL:
- Mortgage loan = EMV − deposit.
- Gross yield, and both mortgage and cash net yield, calculated against EMV (not purchase price) — this applies uniformly, confirmed against the live code.
- Max bid = the MAXIMUM price to pay to hit target. Yield max bid = annual rent / target yield. ROI max bid solved by working back from target money-left-in.

HMO:
- Mortgage fixed at 75% LTV of EMV (not deposit-derived like BTL).
- Bills auto-calculated at 10% of total rent.
- Council tax entered annually, divided by 12 for monthly display.
- Same EMV-based yield convention as BTL. ROI max bid uses an 80-iteration bisection search (stamp duty is banded/non-linear, so no closed form).

SA (Serviced Accommodation):
- Revenue = nightly rate × (occupancy% / 100) × 365.
- Running costs: management/utilities/maintenance as % of revenue (each defaults to 10%, user-editable), plus fixed cleaning and insurance (£/mo × 12), plus council tax (annual, as entered).
- Net annual income (before mortgage) = revenue − running costs.
- Cashflow: net income minus annual mortgage interest (mortgage version); net income as-is (cash version).
- Gross/net yield: EMV-based, same convention as BTL/HMO.
- Total cash invested reuses BTL's exact pattern (deposit/purchase-price-based), extended with SA's own upfront costs.
- Max bid uses BTL's closed-form approach (not HMO's bisection).

Flip (buy-refurbish-sell):
- Cash-only — no mortgage version, no rental income, no yield concept.
- Total cash invested = purchase price + stamp duty + buying fees + refurb + contingency amount. Estate agent fee is deliberately excluded from cash invested (it comes out of sale proceeds, not paid upfront).
- Contingency is optional (% of refurb cost); blank is treated as 0.
- Net profit = sale value − purchase price − stamp duty − buying fees − refurb − contingency amount − estate agent fee amount.
- ROI = net profit / total cash invested. Profit margin = net profit / sale value.
- Max bid solves for the highest purchase price still hitting a target ROI via 80-iteration bisection (purchase price affects stamp duty, which affects both sides of the ROI calc). The search direction was independently verified numerically (ROI strictly decreases as purchase price rises) rather than assumed from HMO's own search. The estate agent fee is fixed to the (fixed) sale value throughout the search, not recalculated per candidate price — deliberate, per the owner's explicit instruction on how a flipper actually reasons about a deal.

AI verdict

One verdict per strategy (BTL, HMO, SA, or Flip), each with its own tailored prompt. Sections in order: VERDICT, RISK FLAGS, STRATEGY FIT, WHAT NEEDS TO CHANGE. Triggered by a button (not automatic), gated to paid users only, capped at 50 analyses per calendar month per user, logged only on a successful Anthropic response (a failed call doesn't count against the cap).

Auth flow

Supabase email + password (not magic-link). Sign up → "check your email to confirm" message shown, user is NOT dropped into the app before confirming. If a login attempt fails specifically because the email isn't confirmed (Supabase's error.code === 'email_not_confirmed'), a "Resend confirmation email" option appears — resend only ever fires in response to that specific failure, never as a standalone/blind form, to avoid enabling spam to arbitrary addresses. Forgot-password flow: request email → Supabase's recovery link (implicit flow, tokens in a URL hash) → the app detects the PASSWORD_RECOVERY auth event and shows a set-new-password panel → success signs the user into the app directly (no second login needed). Confirmed working against the real Supabase project, not assumed. Logout is available from every logged-in page and returns to the landing page.

- `/index.html` — the calculator (BTL/HMO/SA/Flip/Comps tabs).
- `/saved-deals.html` — the user's saved deals, listing + "Add to pipeline" per deal.
- `/pipeline.html` — the kanban pipeline (paid feature, see below).
- `/portfolio.html` — tracking properties actually bought (paid feature, see below).
- `/refurb.html` — cost a refurb line-by-line or as a quick trade-by-trade total, with contingency and VAT (paid feature).
- `/letters.html` — outreach letter templates (empty-property enquiry, follow-up, probate, direct offer) with a live preview, copy, and download (paid feature).
- Logged-in nav, present on all logged-in pages: Home / Calculator / Saved Deals / Pipeline / Portfolio / Refurb Estimator / Letter Templates / Log out.
- Route protection: index.html, saved-deals.html, pipeline.html, portfolio.html, and home.html all use a shared client-side check (auth-guard.js) that redirects to `/` if there's no Supabase session. A forced-open protected page is an empty shell with no data — nothing of value is exposed by bypassing the redirect.

Saved deals

`deals` table (id, user_id, deal_type text — 'btl'/'hmo'/'sa'/'flip', deal_data jsonb, created_at, pipeline_stage text nullable). JSONB + deal_type means new strategies slot in with no schema change (already proven twice, for SA and Flip). Write path is browser → server → Supabase, never browser → Supabase directly, so validation and usage caps live in one place. RLS restricts every row to its own user_id. Free users are capped at 2 saved deals; paid users unlimited — enforced server-side.

Dead-schema note, important: `deals` also has a nullable `address` column (migration run directly in Supabase this session) that is NOT currently written to anywhere. A separate, optional address input was built alongside the save flow, then deliberately reverted in favour of a single combined field (see the save-modal note below) — the column was deliberately left in place rather than dropped, as the intended home for a real structured-address field later (see "structured property address with autocomplete" in Feature ideas below). Don't assume this column holds data — every row's `address` is null.

Deal Workspace (built, saved-deals.html): clicking a saved-deal row opens a modal — index.html's save modal and saved-deals.html's own Move to portfolio modal were the first two modals on this app; this is the third, on saved-deals.html. An **Analysis** section is always free — it reuses compare-deals.js's own `COMPARE_FIELDS`/`fmtCompareField` (the same per-strategy field list and formatter the compare table already uses) rendered as a plain label/value list rather than a comparison table, since it's one deal, not several. Below it, three sections are paid-gated, each independently: **Notes**, **Offer history**, and **Stage**. Notes/offers markup, CSS, and JS (`loadNotes`/`addNote`/`deleteNote`, `loadOffers`/`addOffer`/`updateOfferOutcome`/`deleteOffer`, the `.dd` outcome dropdown + its `handleOfferOutcomeChange` bridge) were copied from pipeline.html's existing details modal essentially verbatim, retargeted from that modal's `detailsDealId` to this one's own `workspaceDealId`, and hit the exact same endpoints (`/api/deals/:dealId/notes`, `/api/notes/:id`, `/api/deals/:dealId/offers`, `/api/offers/:id`) — no new backend work. Stage shows a 5-option dropdown (`POST /api/pipeline/:id/stage`) if the deal is already pipelined, or a single "Add to pipeline" action (`POST /api/pipeline`, same endpoint the row's pre-existing button already used) if it isn't yet; either way it calls `loadSavedDeals()` afterward so the row's own pipeline badge and the modal's own control both refresh in sync.

Plan-gating for this: saved-deals.html previously had no plan awareness at all (it's a free page). It now fetches `GET /api/profile` on load and stores `currentPlan`, purely to decide what these three sections render — the page itself stays otherwise ungated. This is a **new gating shape** for the app: a partial lock *within* one view (three sections behind one plan check, inside an otherwise-free modal), rather than the all-or-nothing whole-page lock every other paid page (pipeline/portfolio/refurb/letters) uses. A free user never fires the notes/offers/pipeline requests at all — gated client-side before any fetch, not just hidden after a 403.

Row action buttons — fast-follow done (a later, separate session): Rename, Move to portfolio, and Delete moved off each saved-deals row into a `.modal-actions` footer at the bottom of the Deal Workspace itself; rows are now purely a click target plus a checkbox, name/meta, and the pipeline badge when applicable — no inline buttons left. The row's own "Add to pipeline" was removed entirely (the function itself, `addToPipeline()`, was deleted, not just its button) rather than moved, since the workspace's existing Stage section (`addToPipelineFromWorkspace()`) already covers the identical action — keeping both would have been a straight duplicate. The three relocated triggers call the same underlying `renameDeal()`/`openMoveToPortfolio()`/`deleteDeal()` functions, unchanged in behaviour, with two additions specific to calling them from inside a modal: `moveToPortfolioFromWorkspace()` closes the Deal Workspace *before* opening the Move to portfolio modal (both overlays share the same z-index, so opening one on top of the other would render the second one invisible/unreachable rather than genuinely stacked — the deal id is captured into a local first, since closing clears `workspaceDealId`); `deleteDeal()` now returns `true`/`false` (purely additive — no other caller existed to be affected) so `deleteFromWorkspace()` can close the workspace only once the delete has actually completed, rather than leaving it open showing a deal that no longer exists. `renameFromWorkspace()` also does its own extra `loadSavedDeals()` call after `renameDeal()` finishes, since that function's own internal refresh isn't awaited — needed to reliably read the new name back into the modal's title afterward. Bulk-select and compare were explicitly NOT touched — they stay list-level, unaffected by any of this.

Comps-in-workspace (built, a later session — Option A, strict point-in-time): a "Comps" section in the Deal Workspace, below Analysis. `deals.comps_snapshot` (jsonb, nullable) holds `{comps, crime, planning, employment, floorArea, selectedTier, asOf}` — captured exactly ONCE, in confirmSaveDeal() (index.html), at the moment of initial save, gated on whether comps had actually been run that session (lastCompsResult truthy); `null` otherwise. `asOf` is a timestamp captured client-side right there (`new Date().toISOString()`), not the deal's own `created_at` column — same moment in practice, but a distinct field, since the client doesn't wait on the server's response before building the snapshot. Included in both the initial and duplicate-confirm-retry POST bodies (same save action), but deliberately NOT re-captured or updated by anything else — there is no re-save/recalculate flow for an existing deal's `deal_data` (checked directly: the only other write to `deal_data` is the rename endpoint's `{...existing.deal_data, name}` merge, which never touches `comps_snapshot` at all), so the snapshot really is fixed at creation, matching Option A's "never updated" design. The workspace renders a condensed summary, not the full calculator view: end value (GDV at the selected tier if a floor area was entered, else the median comp price as a fallback), £/sqm, sold-comparable count, and one-line crime/planning/employment headlines (each independently degrading to "Unavailable" if that piece wasn't available when the snapshot was taken). The section heading itself carries the date ("Comps · as of 12 Aug 2026"), plus a caveat line ("Snapshot from when this deal was saved — may not reflect current market") — deliberately never presented as live data. A `null` snapshot (no comps run before saving, or a deal saved before this feature existed) renders a quiet "No comps were saved with this deal." — not an error, not a CTA. Product reasoning for never refreshing: deals in this pipeline typically live on the order of ~2 months from analysis to completion, so staleness risk from a fixed point-in-time snapshot is low — a deliberate simplicity trade-off, not an oversight.

Property Pack PDF (built, a later session): a "Download pack" action in the Deal Workspace footer (saved-deals.html) generates an investor-ready PDF for that one deal, client-side via jsPDF — same CDN-script pattern letters.html already uses for its own PDF download. Built entirely from data already stored on the deal (`deal_data` via `COMPARE_FIELDS`, `comps_snapshot`, `address`/name) — no live recompute, no calculation logic duplicated or re-derived, so the pack always matches the calculator exactly. Layout: an indigo header band (address/name, strategy, generated date), a dominant headline callout for Max bid (ROI) — the `oR` field every strategy's `COMPARE_FIELDS` list already ends on — with the remaining fields from that same list as a smaller 3-column secondary grid below it, and a Comps section (see below). The ONLY source of green/amber colour anywhere in the pack is the deal's own pre-computed `meetsYield`/`meetsROI` booleans from calc-engine.js — never an invented threshold; every other figure stays neutral ink.

Two follow-up fixes, then a font embed, all in the same feature: (1) metric cards initially clipped long labels (e.g. HMO's "Total investment (mortgage)") outside the card border — fixed by measuring every label with `splitTextToSize()` against the real font/width in use and sizing each card's height from the actual line count returned, rather than a fixed guess; confirmed by measuring every label across all four strategies that this is the only label anywhere that wraps to 2 lines. (2) that fix left the value crowding the label — fixed by computing real visual whitespace from font cap-height geometry (baseline-to-baseline distance isn't the same as visual gap) rather than an arbitrary baseline offset. (3) Sora embedded to match the app's own typeface (previously fell back to jsPDF's built-in Helvetica) — Regular (400) + **Bold** (700, matching `.metric .mv`/`.offer-box .ov` in styles.css, both `font-weight:700` — confirmed from the CSS, not guessed as SemiBold). Google's own font repo stores Sora only as a variable font, which jsPDF can't embed (it needs static single-weight TTFs) — real static instances were sourced instead from Google's CDN (`fonts.gstatic.com`) and verified byte-for-byte to have no `fvar` table before use. Files live in `/fonts/` (`Sora-Regular.ttf`, `Sora-Bold.ttf`, ~32KB each, plus `OFL.txt` for license compliance) and are fetched lazily — only when a pack is actually generated, never on page load — then cached (base64, in memory) for any repeat download in the same session, with the same self-clearing-on-fetch-failure pattern used to fix the earlier Nomis geography cache bug (see Employment feature above), so one failed fetch can't wedge every later attempt. Verified against the real embedded output, not just the code: confirmed the wrap-measurement above still holds identically with the real Sora font in place (character widths differ from Helvetica's but the dynamic measure-and-fit system adapts automatically either way), and decoded the actual rendered PDF text back out via the embedded font's own glyph-to-Unicode map to confirm every label/value/currency symbol renders correctly with nothing garbled.

Comps section: only rendered if `deal.comps_snapshot` is non-null (a numbers-only pack still generates cleanly otherwise — no error, section just doesn't appear), reusing the exact same `computeCompsSummaryRows()` the Deal Workspace's own Comps section already computes from (see "Comps-in-workspace" above), so the two never drift apart. Headed "Comps as of [created_at]" plus a point-in-time caveat — made even more prominent than the in-workspace version, since a PDF leaves the user's hands and the app's own future updates to a live view can't travel with it. Filename is an address-or-name slug + `.pdf`. Not yet built: photo upload — the original backlog entry for this feature envisioned photos as part of the pack, but that dependency was deliberately not waited on; this shipped as a numbers/comps-only pack first, photos can follow as its own later addition.

Saved defaults

`profiles` table holds target_yield, target_roi, default_mortgage_rate, and a standard_fees jsonb (solicitor/mortgage/searches). Shared across BTL/HMO/SA (not Flip — its input set, a single "buying fees" figure and no mortgage rate, doesn't map cleanly onto this shared schema, so Flip has no "save as my defaults"). Saving from any one tab overwrites the same shared row; loading pre-fills all applicable tabs on login. Free to all logged-in users, not plan-gated. A database trigger on auth.users automatically creates a profiles row (plan='free') for every new signup, regardless of signup path.

Pipeline (Pillar 2, first feature — built)

A deal is "in the pipeline" when its pipeline_stage column (on the same deals table — not a separate table, to keep one source of truth) is non-null. Stages, in order: analysing → viewing → offered → agreed → completed. "Add to pipeline" sets stage to 'analysing'; "remove from pipeline" sets it back to null — the underlying saved deal is never deleted, only the stage-tracking state changes. Stage changes happen via a per-card dropdown, or by dragging a card between columns — full desktop HTML5 drag-and-drop (a dedicated draggable handle, not the whole card; drop-zone columns; a custom drag image so the card visibly lifts) plus a hand-reimplemented touch-drag for mobile (native HTML5 DnD doesn't fire on touch devices — a floating clone follows the finger, elementFromPoint() detects which column it's over). Both drag paths and the dropdown all funnel through the same changeStage() call, so there is exactly one place a stage change is ever persisted. Side-by-side comparison lets the user select 2+ pipeline deals (any mix of strategies) and see their key figures in a table, built from a per-strategy field map reading values already computed and stored in deal_data at save time — no calculation logic is duplicated or re-derived for comparison, so figures always match the calculator. The entire pipeline (viewing and modifying) is a paid feature, enforced server-side via the same plan-check helper used by the AI verdict — free/unauthenticated requests are rejected before touching the database, not just hidden client-side.

Each pipelined deal can now carry notes and an offer history, via a "Notes & offers" modal opened per card (pipeline.html had no click-to-detail view before this — this is its first one). Two tables, both linking to a deal via deal_id references deals(id) on delete cascade, RLS-scoped to auth.uid() (db/007_deal_notes.sql, db/008_deal_offers.sql): deal_notes (note_text, created_at) are timestamped log entries with no edit endpoint — to change one, delete and re-add; deal_offers (amount, offer_date, outcome — Pending/Rejected/Accepted/Withdrawn) has an editable outcome via PUT /api/offers/:id, which is why 008 includes an update RLS policy from the start (006 on portfolio_properties missed it originally and edits silently failed — not repeated here). Both sets of endpoints (/api/deals/:dealId/notes, /api/deals/:dealId/offers, /api/notes/:id, /api/offers/:id) share the same auth+plan gate as the rest of the pipeline, and POSTs additionally verify the deal_id belongs to the requester before attaching anything to it.

Payments (Stripe, test mode, live and verified)

Single £29/month subscription. Checkout: POST /api/create-checkout-session creates a Stripe Checkout Session for a logged-in free user, with both client_reference_id and metadata.user_id set to the Supabase user id (redundant on purpose, for whichever field the webhook ends up reading). Webhook: POST /api/stripe-webhook verifies the Stripe signature before looking at anything (rejects with 400 otherwise, never touches the database on an unverified request), mounted with express.raw() and registered before the global express.json() middleware so Stripe gets the exact raw bytes it signed while every other route keeps parsing JSON normally. On checkout.session.completed for a subscription, it flips the matching user's profiles.plan to 'paid' via a service-role client scoped to that one handler only — never exposed to any user-facing route. Redelivered webhook events are naturally idempotent (a plain UPDATE to a fixed value). The Stripe secret key, publishable key, price ID, and webhook signing secret all live in .env, never in the browser or in git.

Comps engine (built)

Server-side sold-price valuation and post-refurb GDV estimation, on its own tab in the calculator. Paid-gated the same way as the Pipeline and AI verdict — auth + plan checked server-side in POST /api/comps, and the EPC API key never reaches the browser (it lives entirely inside epc-floor-area.js). The old `postcodes` table was dropped entirely as part of a re-architecture — at 534MB it was breaching the Supabase free tier's disk allowance and had actually forced the database into read-only mode, so this was a forced fix, not a preference. The subject postcode is now resolved live, per request, via a single call to postcodes.io (postcodes-io.js, free, no API key) — including terminated postcodes (real, retired postcodes with no live geography of their own): postcodes.io's 404 response for one still carries its last-known coordinates, which lookupPostcode() returns directly rather than treating as a failure, and if an MSOA is separately needed for a terminated postcode (the crime feature, below), a reverse-geocode fallback finds the nearest live postcode's MSOA instead of hard-failing. The 0.5-mile radius search then runs directly against HM Land Registry Price Paid Data (PPD) sold prices in `sold_prices`, which carries its own lat/lng columns (db/012_sold_prices_lat_lng.sql), filtered with the same bounding-box pre-filter + exact haversine check the old postcode-table lookup used, just applied to sold_prices directly instead of joining through a list of nearby postcode strings. Historical rows were geocoded once via scripts/backfill-sold-prices-lat-lng.js (bulk postcodes.io lookups, ~408k distinct postcodes); scripts/ingest-sold-prices.js now geocodes every newly-ingested batch inline the same way, so a future PPD import doesn't need a separate backfill pass to become comps-eligible. Each comp is enriched with EPC floor area (exact house-number match; permanent cache in epc_floor_area_cache, including cached "no match found" results, so any given postcode's EPC data is only ever fetched once). Headline valuation is the median sold price, with a 20th/80th percentile range (null below 5 comps — not enough evidence for a range) and a high/medium/low confidence signal based on comp count only (see open items below). A GDV tier picker (50th/75th/90th percentile of floor-area-matched £/sqm comps — conservative/refurbished/best-in-area) lets a sourcer enter a post-refurb floor area and apply the resulting GDV as the end value (EMV, or Sale Value for Flip) on any of the four strategy tabs, via an explicit "Use this GDV" button — never silently auto-filled.

Open items on the comps engine:
- PPD ingest currently only loads a 12-month window (WINDOW_MONTHS in scripts/ingest-sold-prices.js), not the 24 months get-comps.js's COMPARABLE_MONTHS is written for. Widening this needs a manual re-run of the ingest script with WINDOW_MONTHS bumped to 24.
- The recency-based confidence downgrade in computeConfidence() (get-comps.js) is commented out, not deleted, pending that 24-month ingest — right now every comp is "recent" by definition, so the downgrade could never fire honestly.
- PPD refresh is manual (re-running scripts/ingest-sold-prices.js against a newly downloaded yearly CSV) — nothing scheduled.
- Stripe is still in test mode (see above) — relevant here since the comps engine, like the pipeline, is paid-gated.
- postcodes.io has no published hard rate limit, but both the backfill script and the inline ingest geocoding are deliberately paced (bulk lookups, small delay between chunks) as a politeness convention, not a documented requirement.

Crime feature (Stages 1-4, built)

Per-1,000-residents crime rate shown on the Comps results, sourced from Police UK's open bulk data (a June 2026 monthly export) rather than their live per-call API. Four tables: `msoa_population` (ONS mid-2024 population estimates, needed to convert raw incident counts into a per-capita rate), `msoa_crime_rate` + `crime_benchmark_meta` (rate per 1,000, low/medium/high band, national percentile), and `msoa_crime_breakdown` (Stage 4 — 6 grouped categories: violence, ASB, shoplifting, burglary, vehicle, other), all computed by scripts/compute-crime-benchmark.js in the same pass and re-run whenever the underlying police data refreshes — not live per-request. The MSOA for a subject postcode resolves via the same postcodes.io lookup the comps engine uses (see above), including the terminated-postcode reverse-geocode fallback — no separate stored geography column. Percentile is the headline figure (a band alone is too coarse — a moderately-high and a very-high area both read as "High"); band and raw rate are shown as secondary detail. Validated against known Nottingham areas before shipping, not just spot-checked against the formula.

Known limitation: the per-capita rate reads town-centre and retail-heavy MSOAs higher, because recorded crime (shoplifting, ASB) concentrates where people gather, not where they live. This is standard behavior for the metric and matches how professional crime-stats sites present the same data — not a bug in this app's calculation. The caveat text shown alongside the crime figure explains this rather than hiding it.

Stage 4 (crime-type breakdown) makes that caveat checkable instead of just asserted: an expandable section under the crime box, collapsed by default, showing all 6 groups percentage-primary ("Anti-social behaviour — 62% (106)") ordered descending. Validated against Beeston High Street (E02005860) — shoplifting-led, confirming this is what explains that area's high headline percentile, not violence.

Planning constraints panel (built)

Live point-in-polygon lookup against planning.data.gov.uk (free, Open Government Licence, no key), shown under the crime box on Comps results — no storage, resolved fresh per request via GET /api/planning-constraints, reusing the same postcodes.io lookup comps/crime already use (lat/lng only, no MSOA needed; that lookup — lookupPostcode() in postcodes-io.js — also now returns the council name, adminDistrict, from postcodes.io's admin_district field, null for terminated postcodes, used by the Article 4 message below). Six flags: Article 4 direction, conservation area, listed building, flood risk zone, green belt, tree preservation zone — active ones shown first with the constraint's name/reference where the API provides one, "None found" for the rest, always all six rows regardless of how many are active. Any upstream failure (unreachable/timeout/429) degrades to a visible "Planning data unavailable" message rather than breaking comps; a genuine zero-hit result is a different, valid state ("None found" across all six), never conflated with "unavailable". Verified against real live API responses, not assumed: SW1A 1AA (Article 4 + conservation area both active) and NG9 2ES (flood risk zone active, matching its real proximity to the Trent).

Article 4 negative-state copy — resolved: Article 4 coverage on planning.data.gov.uk is nationally incomplete. Confirmed directly (full scan of all 7,276 entities in the article-4-direction-area dataset, plus an independent text search by borough/town name, not just a spot check): Broxtowe Borough Council has zero Article 4 records published anywhere in the dataset, despite NG9 2ES genuinely being in an Article 4 area. So the Article 4 row could show a false "None found" — not a bug in the query, a gap in the government source data itself. Since Article 4 status matters most to sourcers (HMO conversions) and a false negative was the costly failure mode here (worse than an over-cautious flag), that row's negative-state copy was softened and now names the specific local council: "None recorded — Article 4 coverage in this dataset is nationally incomplete; verify with the local council (COUNCIL NAME)." — falling back to "...verify with the local authority." when no council name is available (e.g. a terminated postcode). Gated on c.key==='article4' in renderPlanningResult() (index.html); the other five flags are unchanged, still the plain "None found".

Employment feature (built): a fourth Comps-results area block, below crime and planning constraints — council-wide employment rate (ONS Nomis, dataset NM_17_5, Annual Population Survey, "Employment rate - aged 16-64") plus the top 5 industries by share of local employment (ONS Nomis, dataset NM_189_1, Business Register and Employment Survey, 18 broad SIC-2007 sections; the dataset's own built-in percentage measure returned empty in live testing, so percentages are computed server-side from raw counts instead). Both datasets confirmed live and genuinely keyless — verified with real numbers for Nottingham, Broxtowe, and Westminster, not assumed (a fresh, independent re-check specifically ruled out NM_57_1, which turned out to be "jobs density," a different metric with no industry dimension at all). Geography: postcode → LAD code (postcodes.io's admin_district code, e.g. "E07000172" — lookupPostcode() in postcodes-io.js now returns this as adminDistrictCode, additive alongside the existing adminDistrict name) → a small server-side proxy, nomis-employment.js, which resolves that GSS code to Nomis's own internal geography id (a ~317-row local-authority list, cached in memory since it barely changes) and queries both datasets. Figures are explicitly labelled as local-authority-wide, not street-level ("Employment · [council] · council-wide figures" + a "figures for the whole local authority, not the immediate area" caveat line) — same honesty pattern as the crime feature's own town-centre-bias caveat. Persisted to draft exactly like crime/planning (lastEmploymentResult, restored without re-firing the network call) — not a special case. Degrades gracefully via Promise.allSettled — a Nomis outage never breaks the rest of the Comps tab, same isolation pattern as crime/planning.

A poisoned-cache bug was found and fixed during dedicated failure-path testing (a later, separate session): the in-memory LAD-geography cache in nomis-employment.js only checked whether a promise existed, not whether it had succeeded — so a single failed fetch (an outage, a timeout) got cached as if it were a completed lookup, silently disabling employment data for every user for the rest of the server process's life, with no recovery short of a restart. Timed proof of the bug (0ms responses on the 2nd/3rd calls after a real 82ms first failure, meaning no retry was even attempted) and of the fix (a subsequent call after "recovery" took a real 1605ms and succeeded, with later calls dropping back to ~336ms once genuinely cached) are both in the session, not just asserted. Fix: only a successful load is cached; a rejection clears the cache variable so the next call retries fresh.

Named employers (e.g. "the biggest employer here is Boots") were investigated and deliberately NOT built — legally unavailable for free, not just hard to find. The underlying business register (IDBR) is protected under the Statistics of Trade Act 1947; ONS is barred from publishing individual business names/data without that business's consent. This is a hard legal barrier, not a temporary data gap — confirmed via the Act itself, not inferred from an absence of search results. The closest free alternative (and what's actually built) is industry-by-sector share, which needs no company names.

Draft persistence & scroll-position restore (built)

Feature ideas — researched, with data-source verdicts

From a research session into how other property sites source several often-requested features. All of these are post-design, post-launch candidates — none jump ahead of the current design work. Prioritise by real user demand after launch, not now.

Re-checked via a fresh round of web research on 2026-08-04: several of the "no source" verdicts below were shut down too early and have been corrected (probate, auction-unsold, derelict/empty-homes, selective licensing, schools, employment) — see each entry for its actual confidence level, which varies entry to entry. Dated here so a future reader knows the provenance.

Buildable now, no external data:
- Refurbishment tab: an editable works list (painting, skimming, electrics, plumbing, etc.) with rough costs. Start user-entered; regional price suggestions could follow later. No data dependency.
- Deal comparison for saved deals: a side-by-side view of two saved deals. This would extend the same pattern (the existing per-strategy field-map table-building) to any two saved deals, not just pipelined ones — not a new comparison mechanism.
- Letter templates for empty/derelict property owners: text templates with address merge fields. Straightforward.

Buildable, external data source exists:
- Article 4 (HMO) flag: built — see "Planning constraints panel" section above. (Kept here for the original research provenance: the coverage caveat flagged at research time was confirmed for real during building — Broxtowe has zero Article 4 records despite a genuine designation existing; see the known issue noted in that section.)
- Selective licensing flag: softened from parked (2026-08-04 correction) — two new findings since the "NOT on planning.data.gov.uk" note above. (a) Kamma, a commercial aggregator, has compiled a national licensing dataset (~389k licensed properties across mandatory HMO / additional / selective schemes, built from public registers + council sites + FOI) — a "buy the feed" option that wasn't recorded before. (b) Some councils publish selective-licensing area polygons as live ArcGIS feature services (e.g. Doncaster), queryable point-in-polygon using the exact same technique as the existing Planning constraints panel. Verdict: national commercial feed exists (Kamma) + free per-council ArcGIS polygons for some councils — buildable in the planning-constraints pattern, no longer just parked.
- Council tax band by postcode: paid API only (PropertyData / Homedata / PropertyInsights) — there's no official VOA API, and VOA's own site is scrape-only and rate-limited. Buy the feed rather than build a scraper. Consolidation note (2026-08-04): several of these same commercial APIs (Homedata, Street Data, PropertyData) bundle council tax band together with EPC, floor area, price history, and comparable sales in a single per-address call — so council tax band, rental comparables (see "Buy, don't build" below), and richer property data might end up being one purchase rather than three separate ones. Homedata advertises a free tier worth testing. Observation only, not a decision.
- Crime rate: built — see "Crime feature" section above. (Kept here for the original research provenance: scoped as free via Police UK's open data, shipped using their bulk monthly export rather than the live per-call API, for the reasons given in that section.)
- Probate property finder: corrected (2026-08-04) — The Gazette, the UK's official public record, publishes wills-and-probate notices via a paginated JSON API; each notice carries the deceased's last address, date of death, and a canonical notice URL. This is a live, free, official, address-level feed — the earlier "not a live property feed" verdict was wrong. Caveats kept: it's a notice feed keyed on the deceased (a lead-generation signal, not a for-sale listing), and there are real product/optics/sensitivity considerations in marketing off death records — a positioning question, not a technical one. Confidence: the source is confirmed to exist and be live; the actual ingestion mechanics haven't been built or tested yet.
- Schools (area research/stats, 2026-08-04, downgraded 2026-08-12): parked, deferred — a closer look found DfE's GIAS and Ofsted are bulk CSV downloads (GIAS ~61.56MB, "all establishment data"; Ofsted's ratings published as a separate monthly file, joined by URN), not a live queryable API the way Nomis employment turned out to be. Buildable in principle, but it's a crime-shaped ingest project (local storage, a join, a proximity query built by hand, periodic re-ingest to stay fresh) rather than a quick keyless API call — findable easily enough on Google/GIAS's own site by anyone who wants it directly, so not judged worth the ingest-pipeline effort right now. The original "strongest, most directly buildable item" framing was wrong — it assumed a live API without checking.

Buy, don't build:
- Rental comparables: PropertyData's "quoting rent" is a proprietary dataset built from VOA 2026 rateable values plus MHCLG floor areas, reconciled and market-adjusted — not a technique we're missing, a licensed commercial data product. The real choice is buy their API, or license the same VOA/MHCLG feeds and build the reconciliation ourselves; buying is the sane option.
- Structured property address with autocomplete (2026-08-10): researched for the deals save flow. postcodes.io — the free API this app already leans on for comps/crime/planning — CANNOT do address-level type-ahead; its own autocomplete endpoint only completes postcode strings, not street addresses (it's built on postcode-centroid data, not a premises-level dataset like Royal Mail's PAF). getAddress.io, a former free-ish option, shut down 4 Feb 2026 after losing a High Court database-rights case to Ideal Postcodes/Royal Mail — not usable. Best path if this is ever pursued: Ideal Postcodes (the same team that maintains open-source postcodes.io) — pay-as-you-go, ~2p per completed lookup, free while the user is still typing (a credit is only spent once a full address is picked), no subscription, so no surprise recurring bill. Deliberately NOT built pre-launch — it would be the app's first paid third-party dependency, for what's only an optional convenience field. `deals.address` (see the dead-schema note above) is already sitting ready as the column this would write to.

Researched, no clean data source found — park unless one emerges:
- Auction-unsold finder: corrected (2026-08-04) — EIG (Essential Information Group) is a central database of every UK auction result since 1991, including a dedicated unsold-lots aggregation, so the data does exist centrally (the earlier "not published centrally anywhere" verdict was wrong on that specific point). Verdict: central source exists, commercial, API access unconfirmed — needs verification before this is a real build, not yet buildable.
- Derelict/old property scanner: softened (2026-08-04) — "derelict" still isn't a field in any dataset. However, some individual councils publish address/division-level empty-property data on data.gov.uk (e.g. Northumberland, by electoral division, with length-of-time-empty and council tax band). So: no national address-level feed, but possibly buildable in specific council areas that publish granular data — the same patchy, per-council shape as the Article 4 coverage situation (see Planning constraints panel above).
- Major employers (area research/stats, 2026-08-04, confirmed harder 2026-08-12): park — and not just "no source found." Nomis (see Employment feature above) gives employment by sector and count, never company names. Confirmed why: the underlying business register (IDBR) is legally protected under the Statistics of Trade Act 1947 — ONS cannot publish individual business names/data without that business's own consent. A structural legal barrier, not a gap that might close later. Companies House was also checked and doesn't collect employee-count data at all. How to work with me (the user)

- The user is new to code. Explain what you're about to do in plain English before doing it, and after making changes, say what you changed and why.
- Make one focused change at a time; don't refactor broadly or rename things unprompted.
- Don't touch the calculation logic (see above), and don't invent/guess a formula for a new feature — ask for the exact spec.
- Before anything is pushed to the shared GitHub repo, verify .env is still git-ignored and scan the diff for secrets — flag anything key-related rather than assuming it's handled.
- When adding a new calculation-bearing feature, verify byte-for-byte that existing verified calculation functions are untouched (diff/checksum before and after), and give a fully worked example before considering it done.
